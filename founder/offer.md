# Offer: SMS Buddy Sale-Ready Pack (10 November 2026)

**Result:** the same 20 simulated buyers (seed 330632) went from **1 buy to 6 buys** (5% to 30%) when shown this offer instead of the plain price list.

**Status:** this is a design, not a live offer. Seven of its promises need your yes before anything is published (see "Decisions only you can make").

**Files:**
- `founder/pitch.md`: the new pitch.
- `founder/pitch-v1.md`: the old pitch.
- `founder/panel/` and `founder/panel-v1/`: the two panels.

Simulated buyers lean agreeable, so read 30% as a ceiling, not a forecast. What the re-test is good for is showing *which* objections the offer removed and which it didn't.

**Pay as you go is not part of the offer**: it is reserved for Nanobotz service customers. All prices include tax.

## 3. The stack (as tested)

**Name:** SMS Buddy **Sale-Ready Pack**.

**Core:** your sale announced to your whole customer list by SMS, under your shop's name, in Sinhala, Tamil or English. **LKR 560, tax included: 1,000 SMS, valid for 6 months.**

**Bonuses, each killing one objection:**
1. **Try it first:** 10 test SMS to your own phone before you pay (objection 1).
2. **We set it up for you:** WhatsApp us your list as Excel or CSV; it's loaded the same working day, with help writing your first message (objections 11 to 14).
3. **Clear Sinhala pricing:** up to 70 characters is 1 credit, and the count shows before you send (objection 2).
4. **Customers can reply:** your WhatsApp number at the end of every message, plus an opt-out line on promotions (objections 9 and 18).

**Guarantee:**
- Your business name is approved within 1 working day, or 500 free SMS are added.
- Any message the delivery report shows as failed is credited back.

**Urgency (true):** set up by 10 December to be sending for 12.12. The date comes from the 1-working-day approval plus setup, not from an invented countdown.

**What the guarantee could cost you:**
- **A missed approval:** 500 SMS × your per-SMS cost (from the cost sheet) × the share of signups you miss the deadline for. At a 10% miss rate, that's 50 SMS of cost per signup on average.
- **Credit-backs:** the failure rate × messages sent. Check the cost sheet, and the operators' charging for failed messages, before promising it.

## 5. Re-test: what moved

**Overall: 1 → 6 buys out of 20.**

Buyers who switched to yes:
- P002, P003, P013, P014 and P019.
- P018, the only v1 buyer, still buys.

The bonuses that actually flipped them:
- **The free test** was mentioned by all 6 buyers.
- **Same-day WhatsApp setup** flipped P003, a last-minute planner.
- **6-month validity** flipped P014.
- **The opt-out line** helped P013, who had worried about the law.

By segment:

| Segment | v1 | v2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Physical shops and restaurants | 17% | **67%** |
| Established online stores | 0% | 25% |
| Social-commerce sellers | 0% | 14% |
| Agencies | 0% | 0% |

**Objections that dropped:**
- "Can't test it first" disappeared.
- Approval time is now only a condition ("if it takes over a day, I'd stop") rather than a reason to pass.
- Credit expiry disappeared.
- "How do I upload my list" disappeared.

**Objections that remain:**
- **Habit: 9 of the 14 passes.** WhatsApp is free, or switching provider mid-season is a risk. An offer can't remove this for social sellers who already sell well on WhatsApp. They want **proof**: a seller like them getting orders from SMS.
- **Sinhala length (3 buyers, including 2 who bought).** Real Sinhala sale messages run past 70 characters, so they cost 2 credits. The honest answer is to show it, for example "a typical 2-part Sinhala sale message is LKR 1.12 per customer", or to offer a short-message template that fits in 70.
- **Automation (2 chain stores).** The pitch never mentioned the API. Add it.
- **Agencies (0 of 3).** They need per-client sender names and a volume rate card. That's a different offer, not this one.
- **Price comparers (0 of 3).** They won't move without a lower rate. Leave them: competing on price is a commodity move.

**Price:** buyers priced the 1,000-SMS pack itself.
- Median "a bargain": about LKR 400.
- Median "getting expensive": about LKR 1,000.
- Median "too expensive": about LKR 1,800.

LKR 560 sits comfortably in that range. There's room to *add* to the pack. There's no need to cut its price.

**Panel note:** one buyer card (P015) got the objection "I already have an SMS provider" while the profile said he'd never paid for SMS. The panel tool deals objections at random, so some cards contradict themselves. It didn't change his answer.

## 6. Decisions only you can make

Before any of this goes on the website or in an ad, each promise has to be true:

| # | Decision | Needed for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ~~Can every new account get 10 free test SMS?~~ **Yes, already live** (confirmed 10 November). Still open: which sender name the trial messages show while the business name is pending | Bonus 1, and the biggest single lever |
| 2 | Can you promise sender-name approval within 1 working day? What happens with the operators if you can't? | The guarantee and the 12.12 date |
| 3 | Is it 70 Sinhala/Tamil characters per credit (67 per part for longer messages)? | Bonus 3 |
| 4 | Can the team load lists and help write first messages the same working day? How many shops per day? | Bonus 2. If capacity is limited, say "first 50 shops before 12.12": real scarcity |
| 5 | ~~Can Sale-Ready credits last 6 months?~~ **Better: credits never expire** (confirmed 10 November) | Core |
| 6 | Can failed messages be credited back from the delivery report? | Guarantee |
| 7 | Can promotions carry an opt-out line, and the shop's WhatsApp number? | Bonus 4 |

If you can only say yes to some, the order of impact from the re-test is:
1. Free test.
2. Same-day setup.
3. Approval time.
4. 6-month credits.
5. Sinhala clarity.

## 7. Next round of the offer (not yet tested)

Add three things:
1. **A short "where do your SMS go" data line**, which answers the agency and list-safety worry.
2. **"Also connects by API"**, with a link to the developer page.
3. **A free "SMS vs WhatsApp" tracking tip**: a different discount code in each channel, so the shop sees which one brought the orders.

Then **get one real result**. The quickest source is a Nanobotz client's 12.12 sale, with their permission and real numbers. That's the only thing that moves the 9 habit buyers.
