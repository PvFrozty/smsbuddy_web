| Lens | Score | Vote |

| Monopoly | 3 | PASS |

The three memos are in `founder/board/` (`offers.md`, `monopoly.md`, `product.md`). The brief they read is `founder/board/brief.md`.

The board scored the business as it is offered today, not the ad. All three said the ad is honest and the price is fine.

## Risks more than one member raised

1. **It's a commodity, and on the terms buyers compare it is behind** (all three). Notify.lk, Text.lk and SMSGo.lk sell the same thing to the same buyer. SMSGo is cheaper (0.54 with tax against 0.58). Rivals' credits last 6 months or never expire, against 3 months here. They also offer free trials, published approval times and a WooCommerce plugin. Being a little cheaper at volume isn't a reason to switch.
2. **No way to try it, and no proof it works** (Offers, Product). There's no free test, no delivery figures and no case study. The buyer has to pay before seeing a single message arrive under their own name. This was the panel's top ask (7 of 20 buyers).
3. **Getting started is slow and unclear** (all three). The steps are sign up, verify, request a sender name, wait an unknown time, pay, upload a list, and only then send. An 11.11 ad sends people into that queue a day before the sale.
4. **The real competitor is free WhatsApp, plus whoever already holds the buyer's sender name** (all three). Twelve of 20 simulated buyers passed out of habit.
5. **The money behind the ads is unknown** (Offers, Monopoly). The budget, the cost to win a customer, the margin per SMS and how many accounts top up have not been measured. Spending on ads before those are known is a guess.
6. **Hidden-cost moments** (Offers, Product). Sinhala and Tamil messages use more credits under a rule that isn't published, and credits expire. The panel read both as "the price isn't the real price".

## Where they disagreed

- **Offers and Product** think the fix is a better offer and a better first five minutes: fund it if those are fixed.
- **Monopoly** thinks those fixes are copyable within a month and don't make a durable business. It wants a small market SMS Buddy can own, most likely Nanobotz's own clients, with SMS built into software they already use, before any money goes into ads.
- Both readings can be true: fix the first five minutes so ads stop wasting money, and build the defensible part inside the Nanobotz client base.

## Conditions checklist

Tick these off before scaling ad spend. The skill that tests each one is in brackets.

- [x] **Free test SMS at signup**: already live. Every new account can send 10 free SMS (owner confirmed 10 November). Still to confirm: which sender name the trial messages show while the business name is pending.
- [ ] **A stated sender-name approval time** you can keep, such as one working day, shown in the portal with progress, and a remedy if missed, such as bonus credits. (ops)
- [ ] **The rupee cost of a typical Sinhala and Tamil message** on the pricing page and in the composer before sending. (pricing; needs the real Unicode credit rule)
- [ ] **A named done-for-you "Sale-Ready" setup**: send the list over WhatsApp, the team loads it, writes the first campaign and sends the day-of reminder, with one named owner per buyer's first send. (offer, ops)
- [x] **Credit validity of 6 months or more**: done, better than asked. The owner confirmed on 10 November that credits never expire.
- [ ] **A guarantee you can afford**, such as unused credits returned if delivery reports show messages didn't arrive. (offer, cfo)
- [ ] **Ads sell "be ready for your next sale", not 11.11**, until free tests and the approval time are live. (marketing; the plan already leans this way)
- [ ] **One real case study by 12.12**: a sale where SMS beat WhatsApp, with numbers and the customer's permission. (launch)
- [ ] **Measured economics before scaling ads**: margin per SMS after network charges and tax, cost per account, share of accounts that top up within 14 days, and the most you can pay to win a customer. (cfo)
- [ ] **A small market you can own**: how many Nanobotz clients send SMS today, with whom, and how many would move; and whether SMS can be built into Nanobotz software (POS, CRM, billing) so customers can't easily leave. (competitors, consumer)
- [ ] **Decide what to cut** so a small seller's first send is effortless: put the API, agency multi-brand features and shared credits behind the main path, not in front of it. (offer)

## The strongest version the board can see

This differs from the pitch. The strongest SMS Buddy isn't "cheaper bulk SMS" sold to strangers through ads. It is **the fastest way for a Sri Lankan shop to get its first sale message out under its own name**. A free test arrives on the owner's phone in the first minute. A promised approval time and done-for-you setup over WhatsApp mean the shop is sending before its next sale. Sinhala prices are shown in rupees, and credits don't punish a business that only sends at sale time.

It starts where SMS Buddy has an advantage no rival has: Nanobotz's existing clients, billed after sending, with SMS built into the software they already run. That gives the business a market it can hold. The 11.11 ad and jingle then become the way to reach shops beyond that base, once the first five minutes are worth paying to send people into.

## Update, 10 November: competitor check

`founder/competitors.md` re-checked 10 rivals' own pages. Most of the conditions above are **not differentiators**: rivals already offer them. That makes them catch-up items before ads scale, not reasons to choose SMS Buddy.

- Free test credits: seven rivals.
- A demo sender name: SMSlenz and Text.lk.
- A stated approval time: SMSGo (24 hours) and SMSlenz (1 business day).
- Sinhala at the English rate: SMSlenz.
- Credits that never expire: five rivals.

Update: credits never expire (owner confirmed 10 November), matching the best rivals. Its 0.45 rate at 100,000 SMS is the lowest published package rate, and the owner confirmed on 10 November that all its prices include tax. Saying "tax included" beside every price is a new, quick checklist item. The only openings that hold up are the done-for-you sale campaign, the 100k rate, and the Nanobotz client base. This supports the Monopoly lens's PASS reasoning.
