# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The buyers are easy to find. Sri Lankan sellers who run seasonal sales live on Meta and search "bulk SMS Sri Lanka", and the 11.11, Black Friday and 12.12 dates give a real deadline to sell against.
- Price is not the obstacle. The panel's "getting expensive" point (LKR 0.75) is above every SMS Buddy rate, so there is room to add value without cutting price.
- The dream outcome is concrete and emotional: "your busiest sale", with a sender name in your customers' own language.
- The parts of a strong stack already exist or are cheap: assisted setup, an API, shared credits across brands, a finished ad, and a warm list of Nanobotz clients.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **It is a commodity, and on terms it is the weaker one.** Being cheaper is the only difference, and it is a narrow one: SMSGo charges 0.54 including tax, while SMS Buddy charges 0.58 pay as you go. Competitors offer free trial credits, faster stated approval, and credits that last 6 months or never expire. SMS Buddy's credits expire after 3 months and there is no trial. As offered today, saying no is the sensible choice.
2. **Buyers have little reason to believe it works, and they cannot try it.** There are no results, testimonials or delivery figures, and no free test. Seven of 20 panel buyers said a free test under their own name would change their answer. Proof is the biggest gap in the value equation.
3. **Getting started takes too long and asks too much.** The approval time for a sender name is unknown, and buyers must sign up, wait, then upload a list. Twelve of 20 passed out of habit (WhatsApp is free, or they already use another provider). The pain may be too mild in this market to beat that habit. Market size and growth are open questions.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- How long does sender-name approval really take, and can you put a promise in writing?
- What does a normal 160-character Sinhala message cost in credits?
- What does a customer cost to acquire, and what is the margin per SMS? Can that margin pay for free test SMS and done-for-you setup?
- Do you have one real sale where SMS beat WhatsApp, with numbers?
- Why do credits expire after 3 months when competitors' last longer?
- Could you double the price and still be the obvious choice? Today the answer is no. What would make it yes?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Free test SMS at signup, sent to the buyer's own phone under their own name (or a demo sender name while theirs is approved).
2. A stated approval time, such as one working day, and a remedy if you miss it (for example, bonus credits).
3. The real cost of a typical Sinhala or Tamil message shown on the pricing page.
4. A named, done-for-you "Sale-Ready" pack: setup over WhatsApp, list upload, a first campaign written for them, and a reminder sent on the day.
5. Credit validity matched to competitors, at 6 months or more.
6. A guarantee the business can afford, such as unused credits returned if delivery reports show messages did not arrive.
7. One real case study by 12.12. Market size and acquisition cost measured before the ad budget is scaled up.
