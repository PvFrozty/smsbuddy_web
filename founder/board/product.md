# Product lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The one thing is available and worth having: "Your sale message, under your shop's name, in Sinhala, Tamil or English, on every customer's phone." That fits in one sentence.
- Price is not the problem. LKR 0.45 to 0.58 sits inside the panel's acceptable range and near competitors. There is no subscription to explain.
- The craft in the ad is honest. Every phone screen is labelled "Example message", and the story starts from the customer's moment (announce, remind, confirm orders), not from a feature list.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The first five minutes go nowhere.** Sign up, verify email, request a sender name, wait for an unknown time, pay before trying anything, and only then send. A buyer who arrives from an 11.11 ad on 10 November cannot do what the ad promised. Competitors give trial credits and a demo sender ID. That gap in the experience matters more than any price gap.
2. **Nothing it does brilliantly that others do not.** Notify.lk, Text.lk and SMSGo.lk offer the same core product, and some beat it on trial credits, credit expiry and plugins. The real rival is free WhatsApp. If the product does not have one thing that clearly beats both, the ad budget buys signups that never top up.
3. **The seams show up as hidden costs.** Sinhala messages cost more credits under an unpublished rule. Credits expire after 3 months when competitors' last 6 months or never. Sender approval is handed off to a process the buyer cannot see. Each of these turns a moment of trust into a moment of suspicion, and the panel read them exactly that way.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, why would a shop owner pick this over WhatsApp, or over the SMS provider they already use?
- Walk me through minute one to minute five for a new seller. When does their own phone first buzz with their own message?
- How long does sender approval actually take, who does the work, and what does the customer see while they wait?
- What does a normal 160-character Sinhala sale message cost, in rupees?
- What would you cut? Is it the API, the agency segment, or multi-brand credits, so that the first send for a small seller is effortless?
- Who owns the moment after the send: delivery report, the reminder, and the next top-up?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. A free test SMS sent to the owner's own phone at signup, under a demo or pending sender name, before any payment.
2. A stated, measured approval time that the business keeps, shown in the portal with a progress status.
3. The rupee cost of a typical Sinhala and Tamil message shown on the pricing page and in the composer before sending.
4. Ads that sell "be ready for your next sale", not 11.11, until conditions 1 and 2 are live.
5. Done-for-you setup over WhatsApp as the default first step for small sellers, with one named owner for each buyer's first send.
