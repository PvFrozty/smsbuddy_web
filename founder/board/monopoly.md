# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- The product works across the whole category: a portal, an API, branded sender IDs, Sinhala, Tamil and English, delivery reports, and credits shared across brands. Nothing is missing that a buyer would expect.
- Price is not the problem. The simulated buyers named LKR 0.75 as "getting expensive" and SMS Buddy charges 0.45 to 0.58.
- It has one asset a newcomer would not have: Nanobotz's existing clients, plus billing after sending for clients spending over LKR 50,000 a month. That is the only distribution channel in the brief that does not depend on ad spend.
- The panel gave a clear list of what would win buyers over (free test SMS, a stated approval time, the real cost of a Sinhala message, setup done over WhatsApp). That shows where a different product could start.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **It is a commodity in a crowded market, and on most terms it is behind.** Notify.lk, Text.lk and SMSGo.lk sell the same thing to the same buyer. SMSGo is cheaper (0.54 including tax), credits elsewhere last 6 months or never expire, and rivals offer free trials, published approval times and a WooCommerce plugin. SMS Buddy has none of the four sources of a durable business: proprietary technology, network effects, scale or a brand. The Monopoly lens predicts thin margins and a price war.
2. **The real competitor is free.** 12 of 20 simulated buyers passed out of habit: WhatsApp broadcasts cost nothing, or they already have a sender name with someone else. Switching costs protect the incumbents, not the newcomer.
3. **Distribution depends on paid ads with no known economics.** The ad budget, the cost to win a customer, how many accounts top up and the margin per SMS are all unknown. A first purchase of LKR 560 leaves little room to pay for a click-driven signup. Copying it takes no time: any rival can match a price or a feature within a month.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What do you know about this market that Notify, Text.lk and SMSGo do not? If the answer is "nothing", what is the plan?
- Which small market can you hold more than half of within a year? For example: Nanobotz clients, Sinhala-first social-commerce sellers, or one industry's POS or software.
- How many Nanobotz clients send SMS today, and with whom? How many would move?
- What is the gross margin per SMS after network charges and tax, and what is the most you can pay to win a customer?
- What is the real sender-name approval time, and could you make it faster than any rival's?
- What would make a customer unable to leave in month six: integrations, stored lists, automation, or bundling with Nanobotz software?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable (PASS). What would change the vote: a named small market where SMS Buddy can show it already has or can quickly win most buyers, most likely Nanobotz's own clients with SMS built into software they already use; a measured cost to win a customer below what a customer pays back; and one feature that clearly beats every rival, such as a Sinhala message costing one credit, approval in the same hour, or free test messages sent under the customer's own name.
